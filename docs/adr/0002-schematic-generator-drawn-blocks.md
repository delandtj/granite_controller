# Schematic generator: drawn blocks on hierarchical sheets

**Status**: Accepted
**Updated**: 2026-10-24 (accepted; review asks 1-4 answered yes: sheet-path net names with one relink, six sheets, power symbols at the pins, relink in pcbkit)
**Updated**: 2026-10-24 (built; deviations folded in, see "Built as" at the end)
**Date**: 2026-10-24

Related: docs/adr/0001-48v-power-plane.md (what the plane is),
power/pp-48-2s/tools/gensch.py (what this replaces), docs/handoff.md
(session 2026-10-24).

---

## Context

The PP-48-xS schematic is generated by tools/gensch.py (531 lines).
Every part is shelf-packed into rows and every pin carries a net label.
There are no wires between parts. Power nets are anchored by one
power-symbol cluster at the bottom of the page. The whole plane, 174
parts for the 2-slot board, sits on one A2 sheet.

That was the right first step: the netlist is deterministic from the
Python, ERC is clean, pcbkit mkboard / place.py / the router all work
from the exported netlist, and the two slots are guaranteed identical.
It is also unreadable. The 2026-10-24 render shows a part list with
symbols, not a circuit: no signal flow, no telling which cap belongs
to which pin, three LM5164 stages that do not look like bucks, a
connector nowhere near what it feeds.

Constraints:

- The board is routed. 174 footprints carry the schematic symbol
  paths ("/<symbol uuid>") and the net names the flat sheet produced
  ("/S1_LQ", "/CTRL_SDA", "GND"). The hand-route plans in
  tools/handroute/ and tools/power.py name those nets.
- Three plane sizes (2, 4, 8 slots) come from one generator; the
  slots must stay identical by construction, not by discipline.
- The output is still not source. Edit the Python, regenerate,
  regenerate again without an electrical change and get the same
  file (uuid5 keys, no randomness).
- KiCad 10.0.7 file format (version 20260306); kicad-cli is the only
  KiCad tool in the flow (erc, netlist, pdf). No GUI step.
- Hand-drawing in the KiCad editor is out: the slots would diverge and
  the 4 and 8 slot planes would be redrawn by hand.

---

## Decision

Rewrite the generator as a small drawing engine plus one Python
function per circuit block, emitting a hierarchical schematic:

- A root sheet with sheet symbols for the functional blocks, the
  power anchors, and the inter-sheet nets as labels at the sheet pins.
- One sheet file per block: input, bucks, mcu, link, expanders, slot.
- The slot sheet is ONE file instantiated N times (KiCad's native
  multi-instance sheet). Per-slot differences (INA238 address pins,
  which expander bank drives it) leave the sheet through sheet pins
  and are tied at the root.
- Inside a block the author places symbols at coordinates with a
  rotation; the generator draws the wires from the existing pin -> net
  tables, puts power symbols on power-net pins, hierarchical labels on
  nets that leave the sheet, and refuses to emit a wire that crosses a
  symbol body or another pin.
- Net labels are used only for nets that leave a sheet and on the
  root. Local nets are wires.
- The pin -> net tables of today's gensch.py are kept verbatim. The
  electrical content does not change; a checker proves it by comparing
  the kicadxml netlists before and after.
- A `pcbkit relink` step moves the routed board to the new symbol
  paths and net names in one deterministic pass.

## Architecture Overview

### Component Breakdown

1. **kicad emitters** (`power/pp-48-2s/tools/sch/kicad.py`)
   - What it does: s-expression writers for every schematic item used:
     symbol instance (with rotation and mirror), wire, junction, local
     label, global label, hierarchical label, no-connect, power
     symbol, text, sheet symbol with sheet pins, the lib_symbols block,
     the root and sub-sheet file skeletons with sheet_instances and
     symbol instances per sheet path.
   - Key interfaces: `Sheet(name, file, uuid)` collecting items;
     `sheet.write(path)`; `Symbol(ref, lib, value, fp, at, rot, mirror,
     fields, instances)`; `sheet_symbol(name, file, at, size, pins,
     page)`.
   - Dependencies: the library flattening of today's gensch.py
     (block_at, raw_symbol, flatten, custom_symbol, pins_of) moves here
     unchanged. Pin positions gain the rotation / mirror transform.

2. **drawing engine** (`power/pp-48-2s/tools/sch/draw.py`)
   - What it does: `Block` holds placed symbols and the pin -> net
     tables; `block.finish()` derives everything drawn:
     - power nets (GND, +3V3, +12V, and per-block choices such as VBUS
       or CTRL_3V3) get a power symbol stub at each pin, oriented by
       the pin direction;
     - nets listed as `ports` get a hierarchical label at one pin or
       at a given edge point and a wire to it;
     - every other net is wired pin to pin: an author-given order, each
       leg a straight, L or Z shaped run on the 2.54 mm grid; T joins
       get junction dots;
     - NC pins get a no-connect flag.
   - Lint: a wire leg may not pass through a symbol body box or over
     a pin of another net; labels and power symbols may not overlap
     each other or a body. Violations stop the generator and name the
     net and the symbols so the author moves a part. There is no
     automatic rerouting beyond the L / Z choice.
   - Key interfaces: `b.sym(ref, lib, value, fp, nets, at=(x, y),
     rot=0, mirror=False, fields=..., desc=..., dnp=False)`;
     `b.wire(net, [(ref, pin), ...], via=[(x, y), ...])` to fix the
     order and bends of a local net; `b.port(net, side="left",
     y=...)` to place a sheet pin; `b.power(net, symbol)` to declare a
     net as a power-symbol net for this block.
   - Dependencies: kicad.py for emit and the pin geometry.

3. **blocks** (`power/pp-48-2s/tools/sch/blocks/`)
   - input.py: J1, F1, D1, C1 C2, the VBUS sense divider. Ports:
     VBUS_SNS. Power: VBUS, GND.
   - buck.py: `lm5164(b, u, vout, ...)` drawn as a buck: VIN caps at
     the VIN pin, EN divider, RON, BST cap over to SW, inductor to
     VOUT, feedback divider with the type 3 injection network drawn as
     the datasheet draws it, output caps. Called twice on the bucks
     sheet (3V3 and 12 V aux, with F2, J4, the aux sense divider and
     the two LEDs). Ports: AUX_SNS, V12_OUT is a power net.
   - mcu.py: U3 with its decoupling at the VDD pins, crystal, NRST cap,
     SWD header, run LED, address straps, I2C2 pull-ups, EXP_IRQ
     pull-up. Ports: SCL2 SDA2 EXP_IRQ VBUS_SNS AUX_SNS INT_OUT SCL1
     SDA1 Sn_ID Sn_TEMP Sn_OFF Sn_LCLR for n <= N (the unused slot
     pins stay NC as today).
   - link.py: U4 and U5 drawn with the isolation barrier as a dashed
     line, the controller-side parts (C24 C26 R28 R29 Q1 R30 J2 J3) on
     the right, plane-side pull-ups on the left. Ports: SCL1 SDA1
     INT_OUT. Power: CTRL_3V3, CTRL_GND, +3V3, GND.
   - expanders.py: one MCP23017 per two slots with its decoupling and
     address straps; GPA / GPB pins as ports Sn_PWRD Sn_RSTD Sn_PGD
     Sn_PG Sn_LQ Sn_LED. Count derived from N.
   - slot.py: hot-swap front end left to right (shunts, LM5069, pass
     FET, UVLO / OVLO dividers, timer, PGD, output TVS and cap), the
     INA238 under the shunt, the enable chain and OV latch below, the
     brick socket and its J2 mate in the middle, node connector with
     the photoMOS and LED-sense opto on the right. Ports: OFF LCLR ID
     TEMP PWRD RSTD PGD PG LQ LED SDA2 SCL2 A0 A1 (INA238 address).
     Power: VBUS, +3V3, GND. OUT and NODE are local nets with a
     PWR_FLAG inside the sheet.
   - Each block file is 60-150 lines of placements and a few `wire`
     orders. The pin -> net dictionaries are copied from gensch.py as
     they are.

4. **root generator** (`power/pp-48-2s/tools/gensch.py`, rewritten)
   - What it does: `gensch.py N`; writes the root sheet and the six
     sheet files into the project dir; places sheet symbols in flow
     order (input top left, bucks under it, slots in a row across the
     middle, mcu / link / expanders at the bottom); at every sheet pin
     a short wire and a local label with today's net name (S1_OFF,
     SCL2, ...); ties the per-slot INA238 address pins to GND / +3V3 /
     SDA2 / SCL2 labels per slot index; the power anchors (power
     symbols, PWR_FLAG) as today.
   - Writes the project's `sym-lib-table` entry for pp as today; the
     title block; prints the part and sheet counts.

5. **netlist checker** (`power/pp-48-2s/tools/schcheck.py`)
   - What it does: exports kicadxml from a reference schematic and the
     generated one with kicad-cli, builds (ref, pin) -> net for both,
     derives the net rename map, and fails unless the map is a
     bijection and every (ref, pin) agrees under it. Also compares the
     part fields (Value, Footprint, LCSC, MPN, DNP) per reference.
     Prints the rename map for `pcbkit relink` and for the sed over
     tools/power.py and tools/handroute/*.py.
   - Run against the committed pp-48-2s.xml as reference, then kept as
     the regression check for every later generator change.

6. **board relink** (`~/Electronics/pcbkit/pcbkit/relink.py`,
   `pcbkit relink <netlist.xml> <board.kicad_pcb> [--save]`)
   - What it does: for each footprint, by reference, sets the symbol
     path (sheet tstamps + component tstamp) from the netlist as
     mkboard does, and renames pad nets from the netlist. Tracks, vias
     and zones are renamed through the old -> new map derived from the
     pads (every old net must map to exactly one new net, else stop).
     Zones refilled, DRC run, the report printed. Nothing moves.
   - Dependencies: pcbnew; the same netlist parsing as mkboard.

### Data Flow

```
tools/sch/blocks/*.py  (pin->net tables + placements)
        |
        v
tools/sch/draw.py  -- wires, power stubs, hier labels, lint
        |
        v
tools/sch/kicad.py -- s-expr for root + 6 sheet files
        |
        v
pp-48-2s.kicad_sch + sheets/*.kicad_sch
        |                       |
  kicad-cli sch erc      kicad-cli sch export netlist --> pp-48-2s.xml
                                                             |
                                 tools/schcheck.py (vs the old xml)
                                                             |
                                 pcbkit relink xml board --save
                                                             |
                                 DRC: 0 unconnected, 0 clearance
```

The board flow comment in pcbkit.toml gets `relink` after the netlist
export; mkboard is only for a fresh board.

Net naming after the change (KiCad rules, nothing invented):

| net today      | net after            | why                                   |
|----------------|----------------------|---------------------------------------|
| GND +3V3 +12V  | same                 | global (power symbols)                |
| /S1_OFF        | /S1_OFF              | label on the root at the sheet pin    |
| /SCL2          | /SCL2                | same                                  |
| /CTRL_SDA      | /Link/CTRL_SDA       | lives only on the link sheet          |
| /S1_OUT        | /Slot 1/OUT          | lives only in the slot instance       |
| /FB1 /SW1 ...  | /Bucks/FB1 ...       | lives only on the bucks sheet         |

Roughly 60 of the 110 nets rename. schcheck prints the exact map.

## Alternatives Considered

### Keep one flat sheet, draw it
- **The idea**: same drawing engine, but all blocks on one A1 page;
  no sheets, no sheet pins, no renames, no relink.
- **Optimizes for**: zero migration of the board and the plans.
- **Sharpest tradeoff**: the 8-slot plane is 8 drawn slot blocks on
  one page (A0 or beyond), and the slots are copies with different
  reference numbers rather than one sheet, so a drawing fix is applied
  N times by the generator and reviewed N times by a reader.
- **Bets on**: the plane family staying at 2 slots. ADR 0001 says 2,
  4 and 8.

### Hand-draw once in KiCad, generator keeps only the netlist
- **The idea**: draw the 2-slot schematic by hand in the editor,
  keep gensch.py as a checker that the drawing's netlist matches.
- **Optimizes for**: the best-looking result for one board, fastest
  to a readable sheet.
- **Sharpest tradeoff**: two slots drawn by hand diverge, and the 4
  and 8 slot planes are redrawn by hand. The checker catches drift
  after the fact instead of making it impossible.
- **Bets on**: never building the 4 and 8 slot planes.

### Automatic placement and routing of the schematic
- **The idea**: the generator places symbols by a force-directed or
  grid heuristic from connectivity and routes the wires with a
  channel router; the author writes no coordinates.
- **Optimizes for**: zero per-block layout work.
- **Sharpest tradeoff**: schematic readability is about convention
  (inputs left, outputs right, decoupling at the pin, a buck drawn as
  a buck) and no small heuristic knows the conventions; the code is
  larger than the six blocks it would replace and the output is still
  judged by eye.
- **Bets on**: a generic router beating a human layout of 15 parts.
  It does not.

### A third-party schematic library (skidl, kicad-skip, kiutils)
- **The idea**: build the netlist in skidl or edit s-expressions
  through kiutils / kicad-skip instead of writing the format by hand.
- **Optimizes for**: not owning the file format.
- **Sharpest tradeoff**: skidl's schematic drawing is immature and
  its placement is the automatic approach above; kiutils / kicad-skip
  are parsers, not drawers, and lag KiCad releases. The format
  surface this generator needs is ten item kinds, all already emitted
  by gensch.py today except sheet, sheet pin and hierarchical label.
- **Bets on**: KiCad 10's format being stable for the ten item kinds.
  It has been since KiCad 6 for all but the instances block.

## Consequences

### Positive
- A reviewer reads the plane as circuits: three bucks, a hot-swap
  front end, an isolated link, in the order the power flows.
- One slot drawing for every plane size; a slot fix is one edit.
- The electrical content is proven unchanged by schcheck, so the
  rewrite cannot silently alter the routed board.
- Per-slot variations (INA238 address) become visible at the root
  instead of being buried in a Python expression.

### Negative
- About 60 net names change on the board, in tools/power.py and in
  the historical handroute plans. One sed from the printed map, but
  the plans then no longer replay against a board generated by the old
  flow. They are history already (the board is routed).
- Each block costs an hour or two of placement work and a few lint
  iterations; the whole rewrite is a working day plus review.
- Two tools to learn instead of one: the block API and relink.

### Risks
- The KiCad 10 syntax for sheets, sheet pins and the per-path symbol
  instances is emitted by hand. Mitigation: validate every file with
  kicad-cli (erc, netlist export, pdf export) in the generator's own
  test, and compare against a two-sheet file saved from the KiCad
  editor once.
- The wire lint is geometric and conservative; it may reject layouts
  a human would accept, costing iterations. Mitigation: `via` points
  on `wire` give the author full control of any leg.
- relink renames tracks through the pad map; a track touching no pad
  (none exist after prune, by definition) would keep its old net.
  Mitigation: relink lists tracks whose net is not in the map and
  stops.
- KiCad may re-annotate a shared sheet when it finds references it
  does not expect. Mitigation: the instances block names every
  reference per path, which is exactly the editor's own format; ERC
  via kicad-cli is the proof.

## What an Expert Would Ask

**Q: The slot sheet is shared by N instances, but slot k's INA238
address pins and the expander bank it hangs off differ per slot. How
does one file express that?**
A: It does not. Anything that differs per slot leaves the slot sheet
as a sheet pin (A0, A1, and the six expander signals) and is tied at
the root per instance, by a label to GND / +3V3 / SDA2 / SCL2 or to
the right expander port. The slot file holds only what is identical.
If a future difference cannot be expressed as a net at a pin (a
different part per slot), the slot sheet would have to split into two
files, and this ADR would be revisited.

**Q: What happens to the routed board when the symbol paths and 60
net names change?**
A: `pcbkit relink` rewrites both in one pass from the new netlist, by
reference. The pad renames come from the netlist; the track, via and
zone renames come from the old -> new map those pads imply, which
must be a bijection or the tool stops. The proof is the DRC after
relink: 0 unconnected, 0 clearance, same as before. The board's copper
does not move. The handroute plans and power.py get the same map
applied by sed, in the same commit.

**Q: Why not keep the old net names by putting labels for every net
at the root?**
A: That is today's schematic with extra steps. A net name with the
sheet path in it ("/Slot 1/OUT") is the KiCad convention and tells a
reader where the net lives. The rename is a one-time cost handled by
relink; keeping flat names forever would mean every local net has a
label somewhere, which is the clutter this ADR removes.

**Q: Who draws the wires without them crossing everything?**
A: The author, by placing parts so that pins face each other, and by
giving `wire` a pin order and bend points where the default L / Z leg
is wrong. The generator does not route; it draws the leg asked for
and refuses to draw one that crosses a body or a foreign pin. This is
deliberate: the blocks are 10 to 60 parts, placement is a one-time
job per block, and a refusal with the net name is cheaper than a
router that produces a crossing a human then has to find.

**Q: Will a regenerate with no electrical change still produce a
byte-identical file, so that git diffs show only real changes?**
A: Yes. Every uuid is uuid5 of a stable key (sheet name, reference,
pin, net plus position for labels and wires), the title block date is
the only free field and it is pinned in the generator. The checker's
first test is: regenerate twice, diff is empty.

**Q: The 8-slot plane's MCU uses pins for S5-S8 that the 2-slot plane
leaves NC, and has four expanders. Does the root sheet still read?**
A: The mcu sheet's ports and the expander count are derived from N,
as today. The root places slot sheets in a row of up to 4 and wraps,
expanders under them. An 8-slot root is a 2 x 4 grid of slot sheets
with the rest below; it is read in the editor by descending into
sheets, not by printing one page. The root carries no circuitry
except the anchors and the address ties, so its density is bounded by
the sheet pin count, about 20 per slot.

## Implementation Plan

### Decisions you will probably want to tweak

- **Internal nets take the sheet path** ("/Slot 1/OUT").
  - Alternative: root labels for every net, keeping today's names.
  - Cost to change later: none before relink runs; after it, a
    second relink and sed. Decide before the first relink.

- **Author-placed coordinates, generator-drawn L / Z wires, lint.**
  - Alternative: a cell grid (`at=("col 3", "row 2")`) with
    symbol-size-aware spacing; or full automatic routing.
  - Cost to change later: the block files are rewritten; the engine
    and emitters stay. A day.

- **Six sheets: input, bucks, mcu, link, expanders, slot.**
  - Alternative: four (input and bucks together, mcu and link
    together); or one per buck.
  - Cost to change later: moving a block between sheets renames its
    internal nets (relink again) and moves its root labels. Half a
    day plus the relink.

- **Power nets as power symbols at every pin**, including VBUS inside
  the input and slot sheets and CTRL_3V3 / CTRL_GND on the link
  sheet.
  - Alternative: drawn rails (one horizontal VBUS wire with caps
    hanging off it), labels for VBUS.
  - Cost to change later: per block, an hour; it is a `b.power`
    declaration versus a `wire` order.

- **Sheet pin names are the slot-local names** (OFF, LCLR, A0), the
  root label carries the index (S1_OFF).
  - Alternative: Sn_ prefixed pins on the sheet. Not possible with
    one shared file, so this is only tweakable if the shared-sheet
    decision is reversed.

### Known unknowns and how the plan absorbs them

- **Exact KiCad 10 syntax for sheet, sheet pin, hierarchical_label
  and multi-path instances.** Default: emit what KiCad 10.0.7 writes
  (confirmed by saving a two-sheet, two-instance test project from the
  editor once and diffing). Pivot signal: kicad-cli erc or netlist
  export rejecting the file, or the editor re-annotating on open.

- **Pin position transform under rotation and mirror.** Default:
  KiCad's convention (symbol y axis up, instance y axis down, rotation
  counter-clockwise in symbol space, mirror before rotate), verified
  by placing one resistor at each of the 8 orientations and comparing
  the netlist pin positions with a file saved from the editor. Pivot
  signal: any wire that lands beside a pin in the ERC report.

- **Whether the lint is too strict to be livable.** Default: body box
  plus foreign pin check, hard failure. Pivot signal: more than a
  handful of `via` points per block to satisfy it; then relax body
  boxes to the drawn outline rather than the pin extent.

- **Net count and name map.** Default: whatever schcheck prints.
  Pivot signal: a net that maps to two names (a net that today spans
  what become two sheets without a port) means a missing port, not a
  checker bug; add the port.

### The mechanical work

- `tools/sch/kicad.py`: move the lib parsing and emitters out of
  gensch.py; add rotation / mirror to the symbol emitter and to
  pin_abs; add junction, hierarchical_label, sheet, sheet pin, the
  per-path instances block, the sub-sheet skeleton. Test: a two-sheet
  file with one shared slot sheet and two instances passes
  kicad-cli erc and netlist export, and references per instance come
  out as written.
- `tools/sch/draw.py`: Block with sym / wire / port / power / finish,
  the L / Z leg builder, junction detection, the lint. Test: a block
  with a deliberate crossing fails with the net name; the same block
  with a `via` passes.
- `tools/sch/blocks/`: six block files; the pin -> net tables copied
  verbatim from gensch.py, including BASIC, the LCSC / MPN fields,
  DNP and descriptions. Test: schcheck against the committed
  pp-48-2s.xml, zero field differences, rename map printed.
- `tools/gensch.py`: root sheet; N handling (MCU NC pins, expander
  count, slot grid, address ties); the title block. Test: N=2 ERC
  clean; N=8 generates and ERC clean (no board).
- `tools/schcheck.py`: as specified; also "regenerate twice, empty
  diff".
- `pcbkit relink`: as specified; test on a copy of the routed board:
  0 unconnected, 0 clearance, 174 footprints relinked, map printed.
- Migration commit: regenerated schematic and sheets, relinked board,
  sed over tools/power.py and tools/handroute/*.py, pcbkit.toml flow
  comment, docs/handoff.md. ERC and DRC output in the commit message.
- Remove the old shelf-packing code; the netlist is the only artifact
  the rest of the flow consumes and it is proven equal.

Review asks:

1. Internal nets take the sheet path, and the routed board is
   relinked once: yes, or keep flat names with root labels?
2. Six sheets as listed, or merge input + bucks and mcu + link into
   four?
3. Power nets as power symbols at the pins (KiCad convention, VBUS
   included), or VBUS drawn as a rail in the input and slot sheets?
4. relink lives in pcbkit (reusable for the controller board) rather
   than in tools/: yes or no?

## Built as (deviations from the text above, folded in 2026-10-24)

- KiCad applies the mirror AFTER the rotation (lib y flipped, rotate
  counter-clockwise, then mirror). The "mirror before rotate" default
  under Known unknowns was wrong; the 12-orientation netlist test
  caught it.
- A local net made only of wires is named "Net-(C102-Pad1)" by KiCad,
  not "/Slot 1/NODE". finish() therefore puts one small local label on
  every local net (on its longest horizontal wire); b.name() moves or
  suppresses it. "Local nets are wires" reads "wires plus one name
  label".
- A power symbol wired only to a PWR_FLAG is "pin not connected" to
  KiCad 10 ERC. The root anchors carry a global label on the wire
  (symbol, label, flag), as the flat generator's clusters did.
- The shared slot sheet cannot hold per-slot text: J101 / J201 Value
  is "NODE" (was "NODE 1" / "NODE 2") and the INA238 description says
  "0x40 + slot - 1" instead of the literal address. schcheck reports
  these four field differences; every (ref, pin) -> net is identical.
- NODE has no PWR_FLAG: X1.8 is a power-output pin, a flag there is an
  ERC error. OUT keeps its flag. The flat generator flagged only OUT.
- The INA238 symbol names pin 1 A1 and pin 2 A0; the slot ports carry
  the pin names and the root ties pin 1 to GND x4 / +3V3 x4 and pin 2
  to GND / +3V3 / SDA2 / SCL2 by slot index, exactly as before.
- Reference / Value placement by free side: a part with no pins on
  top gets both above the body; else below; else beside; a part with
  pins on all four sides gets them at its top-right corner. Two-pin
  parts keep the KiCad convention (reference above, value below, or
  both to the right when upright). The lint does not check text.
- The LED cathode: Device:LED's arrow graphics reach past the K pin,
  so a wire entering its own pin along the pin axis is exempt from the
  body-crossing lint.
- Net names after the build: 71 renamed, the map is what schcheck
  prints; /VBUS, /CTRL_3V3, /CTRL_GND became the global VBUS,
  CTRL_3V3, CTRL_GND (power symbols). tools/power.py and the handroute
  plans were rewritten with the map; pcbkit.toml power_nets was left
  as it was (KRT routing is history for this board).
- The 8-slot root generates and passes ERC except for 8 dangling
  labels S5..S8 ID / TEMP: the STM32G0B1 pin table has ADC inputs for
  four slots only, as in the flat generator. A design gap of the
  8-slot plane, not of the generator.
